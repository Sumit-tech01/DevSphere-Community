# DevSphere Coding Challenges

DevSphere challenges are practical, free, and open to everyone. Each challenge gives you a clear problem, a set of requirements, optional bonus goals, and a rubric so you can evaluate your own work before sharing it.

Challenges are for learning. The goal is not to produce a perfect submission; it is to finish something, explain your decisions, and learn from other approaches.

## How the challenge system works

1. **Read the brief.** Each challenge includes requirements and a definition of done.
2. **Plan before coding.** Sketch the layout, choose the data, and decide how you will test it.
3. **Build in your own repository.** Do not commit solutions to this repository.
4. **Check the rubric.** Use it to review your own work before submitting.
5. **Submit in Discussions.** Post in **Project Showcase** using the requested title format.
6. **Share your reflection.** Explain what was difficult, what you learned, and what you would change.
7. **Give feedback.** Review at least one other submission with specific, kind comments.

New challenges are announced in **Announcements** and discussed in **Hackathons & Challenges**. There is no fixed global deadline unless a challenge states one; threads remain open for future learners.

## Submission format

Create a **Project Showcase** discussion with this title:

```text
[Challenge 001] Your Name — Developer Profile Card
```

Include:

- Repository link.
- Live demo link, if available.
- One or two screenshots.
- Technologies used.
- The requirements you completed.
- The bonus features you attempted.
- A short reflection: what you learned, what was hard, what you would improve.
- Any specific feedback you want.

Do not include private contact information, credentials, or someone else's personal data.

## Evaluation criteria

Challenges use a 100-point rubric. The exact weights may vary by challenge, but the standard categories are:

| Category | What reviewers look for |
| --- | --- |
| **Requirements** | All core requirements are implemented and working |
| **Responsive design** | Layout works at small, medium, and large widths without horizontal scrolling |
| **Accessibility** | Semantic HTML, keyboard access, focus states, contrast, alt text, and reduced-motion support |
| **Visual design** | Clear hierarchy, deliberate spacing, consistent typography and color |
| **Code quality** | Readable structure, meaningful names, no unnecessary complexity |
| **Communication** | README, screenshots, and reflection explain the work clearly |

A submission does not need to use a framework, a build tool, or a paid service. Simplicity is a strength when it is intentional.

## Rules

- **Original work.** You may use documentation, tutorials, and design references, but the implementation must be yours. Credit any assets or code you reuse.
- **Free tools only.** Do not require paid software, paid APIs, or a paid hosting plan.
- **Respect privacy.** Use your own information only if you are comfortable sharing it, or use clearly fictional data. Never publish someone else's personal data.
- **Accessibility matters.** A visually attractive card that cannot be used with a keyboard is incomplete.
- **Disclose AI assistance.** If you used AI tools for substantial parts of the work, say so and make sure you can explain the result.
- **No harassment or vote manipulation.** Feedback should help the builder improve.
- **Follow the Code of Conduct.** All community policies apply.

## Feedback guidelines

Good feedback is:

- **Specific:** point to the behavior, line, or design decision.
- **Actionable:** suggest a next step.
- **Balanced:** acknowledge what works.
- **Respectful:** critique the work, not the person.
- **Optional:** do not rewrite someone's project unless they ask.

Example:

> The card stays readable at 320px, and the focus ring is clear. The skill list becomes cramped below 360px; consider wrapping it or switching to a two-column layout. The heading hierarchy is clear, but the link labels could be more descriptive.

## Proposing a challenge

Use [CHALLENGE_TEMPLATE.md](CHALLENGE_TEMPLATE.md) and open a **feature request** or **General Discussion** post with:

- The problem statement.
- Target skill level.
- Core requirements.
- Bonus goals.
- Suggested timebox.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Any safety, privacy, or accessibility considerations.

Challenges should be buildable with free tools and should not require access to private data or a commercial account.

## Recognition

Completed challenges may be highlighted in a community update or showcase thread. Recognition is based on completed work and thoughtful reflection, never on follower counts or paid promotion.
