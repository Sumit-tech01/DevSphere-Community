# AI & Machine Learning — What Are You Learning, Building, or Questioning?

AI and machine learning move quickly. New models, tools, benchmarks, and claims appear every week. In the middle of the noise, it helps to have a place to think carefully with other builders.

This is that place.

You do not need to be a researcher to participate. Students, application developers, data scientists, product builders, and curious beginners are all welcome.

## What to share

### 1. What you are learning

- A concept that finally clicked.
- A paper or course that helped.
- A visualization or explanation that made something clearer.
- A topic you are struggling with.

### 2. What you are building

- A model, agent, RAG pipeline, evaluation harness, or data tool.
- A small experiment that did not go as planned.
- A lesson about latency, cost, data quality, or deployment.

### 3. What you are questioning

- A claim that needs evidence.
- A benchmark that does not match real-world use.
- A trade-off between capability, cost, privacy, and safety.
- A failure mode that deserves more attention.

## A good AI discussion includes context

When sharing work or asking for help, include:

- **The task:** what the system is supposed to do.
- **The data:** source, size, licensing, and limitations (without sharing private data).
- **The model or method:** name and version.
- **The evaluation:** metrics, validation strategy, and baseline.
- **The failure cases:** where it breaks and why that matters.
- **The constraints:** latency, cost, hardware, privacy, or regulatory context.

Avoid screenshots of a single impressive output as proof of quality. Show the evaluation, not only the demo.

## Discussion starters

- What is one ML concept you wish you had learned earlier?
- What is the most useful evaluation metric for your current project, and why?
- Have you built a RAG system? What did you measure, and what failed?
- Which paper changed how you think about your work?
- How do you decide between prompting, fine-tuning, and retrieval?
- What is a responsible-AI question your team has not answered yet?
- What is a popular AI claim you think needs more evidence?

## Community norms

- **Separate evidence from opinion.** Label both clearly.
- **Cite sources.** Link to papers, documentation, or reproducible experiments.
- **Do not share private data.** Redact logs, datasets, and prompts.
- **Do not post hype as fact.** "It works" needs a definition of "works."
- **Be patient with beginners.** Confusion is part of learning.
- **No fearmongering or doom loops.** Discuss risks seriously, without derailing every thread.
- **Disclose AI assistance.** If AI helped write a substantial post or piece of code, say so and verify it.
