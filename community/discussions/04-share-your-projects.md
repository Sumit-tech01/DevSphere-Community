# Share Your Projects — Show the Community What You Are Building

Every project starts somewhere: a messy prototype, a weekend experiment, a tool built to solve a personal annoyance, or a product with real users.

This is the place to share it.

You do not need a perfect launch, a large audience, or a polished landing page. Share what you have, explain what you are trying to do, and tell people how they can help.

## Project template

```markdown
## Project name

One sentence describing what it does.

## The problem

Who is it for, and why does it matter?

## What I built

The main features, architecture, and notable technical decisions.

## Links

- Repository:
- Live demo:
- Screenshots:

## Stack

Languages, frameworks, databases, and infrastructure.

## What stage is it at?

Idea / Prototype / MVP / Live / Maintained

## What feedback do you want?

Be specific: UX, performance, code structure, naming, accessibility, positioning, or something else.

## What is next?

Known limitations and the next milestone.

## Are you looking for collaborators?

Yes or no. If yes, what kind of help do you need?
```

## What makes a showcase useful

- **Context.** Explain the problem before the technology.
- **Honesty.** Say what works and what does not.
- **Links.** Give people a way to try the project or read the code.
- **Specificity.** Ask for the feedback you actually want.
- **Visuals.** A screenshot or short demo helps people understand quickly.
- **Accessibility.** If the project is public, mention how you tested it, or ask for help with it.

## What to avoid

- Dropping a link with no explanation.
- Reposting the same project repeatedly without meaningful updates.
- Hiding that a project is commercial or that you are affiliated with it.
- Asking people to star, follow, or upvote without engaging with their work.
- Sharing private data, credentials, or someone else's personal information.

## Giving feedback

Good feedback is specific and actionable.

Instead of:

> "Looks nice."

Try:

> "The onboarding flow is clear. On the settings page, the save button is hard to find at mobile widths because it sits below a long scroll. Consider making it sticky or moving it to the header."

When you receive feedback, assume good intent, ask clarifying questions, and decide what is useful. You do not have to implement every suggestion.
