# Find Project Collaborators — Build Something Together

Great projects rarely happen alone. Sometimes you need a second pair of eyes, a designer, a backend developer, a technical writer, or someone who shares the problem you are trying to solve.

This is the place to find collaborators and to offer your own skills.

## Collaboration template

```markdown
## Project

Name and one-sentence description.

## The problem

Who is it for, and why does it matter?

## Current stage

Idea / Research / Prototype / MVP / Live / Maintained

## What I need help with

Specific tasks or roles, not a vague list of skills.

## Skills that would help

Required and nice-to-have skills.

## Time commitment

Estimated hours per week and expected duration.

## Collaboration model

Volunteer / Open source / Revenue share / Paid / Other

## Ownership, credit, and licensing

How contributions will be credited and licensed.

## How to express interest

A discussion reply, a GitHub issue, or an application link. Do not ask for private documents up front.

## Links

Repository, demo, design, or project brief.
```

## Make your request clear

A strong collaboration post answers:

- **What** are we building?
- **Why** does it matter?
- **What** needs to be done?
- **Who** would be a good fit?
- **How much** time will it take?
- **What** is the collaboration model?
- **How** will credit and ownership work?

If you are not sure yet, say so. Clarity about uncertainty is better than false confidence.

## If you are offering your skills

Tell people:

- What you are good at and what you are learning.
- What kind of project interests you.
- How much time you can commit.
- Whether you want to volunteer, learn, or be paid.
- Whether you have a portfolio or examples.

You do not need to be an expert to contribute. Reliability and communication matter as much as technical skill.

## Safety and fairness

- **Do not ask for money up front.** Legitimate open-source collaboration does not require a "joining fee."
- **Do not require private documents** such as identity cards, bank details, or passwords.
- **Be honest about compensation.** If the work is unpaid, say so clearly.
- **Agree on ownership and credit early.** Use a license and a simple written agreement for commercial work.
- **Respect people's time.** Respond to applications, even if you choose someone else.
- **Follow the Code of Conduct.** Collaboration is still community participation.

## For open-source projects

If you need contributors, make the entry point easy:

- Label beginner-friendly issues.
- Write a clear `CONTRIBUTING.md`.
- Explain how to run the project locally.
- Offer a small, well-scoped first task.
- Be responsive and kind in review.

You can also share the opportunity in the **Open Source** discussion thread.

## A note on finding a co-founder

A co-founder is not just a collaborator. It is a long-term commitment with legal, financial, and interpersonal consequences. Take time to understand each other's goals, working style, and expectations. Do not pressure someone into a commitment, and do not promise funding or success you cannot guarantee.
