# Hackathons & Competitions — Find a Team, Share an Event, Tell the Story

Hackathons are one of the fastest ways to learn: a deadline, a team, an unfamiliar API, and a demo that has to work.

This category is for discovering events, forming teams, sharing preparation tips, and reflecting on what happened after the deadline.

## Share an event

Use this format:

```markdown
## Event name

## Organizer

## Date and time zone

## Format

Online / In person / Hybrid

## Eligibility

Who can participate? Students, professionals, specific regions?

## Cost

Free / Paid / Unknown — be explicit.

## Theme or tracks

## Team size

## Registration link

Official source only.

## Deadline

## Why it is interesting

One or two sentences.

## Are you looking for teammates?

Yes or no. If yes, what skills do you need?
```

## Before you post an event

- Link to the **official event page**, not a referral or shortened link.
- State the cost clearly. DevSphere does not promote events that require undisclosed fees.
- Include the time zone and deadline.
- Check that the event is legitimate and currently open.
- Disclose whether you are affiliated with the organizer.
- Do not post the same event repeatedly.

DevSphere does not automatically endorse an event because it appears here. Verify the organizer, rules, eligibility, and data policy yourself.

## Find a team

If you are looking for teammates, reply with:

- Your skills and experience level.
- The track or idea you want to pursue.
- Your time commitment and availability.
- Whether you are open to joining an existing team or starting one.
- A way to contact you through GitHub or the event platform.

Keep personal contact details out of public posts. Use the event's official team formation tools when possible.

## Team formation guidelines

- Agree on the problem and scope before dividing work.
- Choose a realistic MVP; hackathon time disappears quickly.
- Define who owns the demo, the repository, and the submission.
- Make sure everyone can explain the project.
- Credit every team member.
- Respect the event's code of conduct and intellectual property rules.
- Do not pressure teammates to work beyond their availability.

## After the event

Share what you built and what you learned:

- The problem and approach.
- What worked.
- What failed.
- The most useful thing you learned.
- Whether the project will continue.
- What you would do differently.

A failed demo is still a useful story. The community learns from the decisions, not only the trophy.

## Discussion starters

- What is the best hackathon project you have seen, and why?
- How do you scope a project for a 24-hour event?
- What is your team formation checklist?
- How do you prepare a demo that works under pressure?
- What did your last hackathon teach you about teamwork?

## A note on prizes and claims

Do not promise prizes, funding, or job offers on behalf of an event unless you are an authorized organizer. Do not post fake competitions or recruit people into pay-to-enter schemes.
