# DevSphere Collaboration Guide

DevSphere exists so developers can build together. This guide explains how to propose a project, find collaborators, set expectations, request a code review, share a showcase, and close a collaboration without ambiguity.

> **Core rule:** never imply payment, ownership, credit, or commitment that has not been explicitly agreed. A public post is a conversation starter, not a contract.

## Before you post

Answer these questions for yourself:

1. **What are you building, and why?** One paragraph is enough.
2. **What stage is it in?** Idea, prototype, MVP, or production.
3. **What exactly do you need?** A skill, a review, a decision, a tester, or a teammate.
4. **What can you offer?** Time, code, design, testing, documentation, mentorship, or compensation.
5. **What is the commitment?** Hours per week and expected duration, with uncertainty stated.
6. **How will decisions and credit work?** Repository ownership, license, authorship, and recognition.
7. **What is out of scope?** Protect the project and the collaborator from scope creep.
8. **Is this safe to discuss publicly?** Remove credentials, private data, customer information, and unreleased confidential material.

If you cannot answer these questions, write a proposal first. That is a useful contribution on its own.

## Opportunity types

DevSphere does not provide legal, tax, or employment advice. The table below is a transparency aid, not a contract.

| Type | What it means | Must be explicit | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Paid employment** | An organization hires someone under an employment agreement | Role, compensation, location, eligibility, process, deadline, employer identity | Fake recruiters, unpaid "trials," requests for money or identity documents before a verified offer |
| **Paid contract or freelance** | A defined scope is delivered for agreed compensation | Scope, rate or budget, currency, payment schedule, revisions, ownership, termination | Unclear scope, "payment after funding," unpaid discovery work |
| **Unpaid / voluntary** | No money changes hands; the work is voluntary | No-payment statement, time expectation, credit, license, expenses policy | Pressure, guilt, hidden employment, vague promises of future pay |
| **Open-source contribution** | Work contributed to a project under its license and governance | License, contribution guide, review process, maintainer approval, credit norms | Assuming a pull request will be merged, expecting payment from volunteers |
| **Equity or revenue share** | Compensation depends on future business outcomes rather than cash now | Written terms, vesting or split, decision rights, exit conditions, legal review | Treating equity as guaranteed income, undocumented promises |
| **Hackathon team** | A time-bound team formed for an event | Event rules, team roles, intellectual-property terms, submission ownership | Missing deadlines, unclear who submits, conflicts over credit |

### Paid opportunities

A paid post should include:

- Organization or individual identity and a link to an official source.
- Role, scope, and deliverables.
- Compensation amount or range, currency, and payment schedule.
- Employment or contractor relationship.
- Location, remote policy, time zone, and eligibility.
- Application or selection process and deadline.
- Whether the poster is the employer, a recruiter, or sharing on behalf of someone else.

Do not post "opportunities" that require an upfront fee, purchase, deposit, or unpaid labor disguised as a trial. Do not ask for bank details, government identity documents, or passwords in a public discussion.

### Unpaid and voluntary opportunities

Unpaid work is legitimate only when it is clearly voluntary. State:

- **"This is unpaid."** Put it near the top, not in the last line.
- The expected time commitment and duration.
- What credit, portfolio use, or public recognition is offered.
- Who owns the work and under what license.
- Whether expenses are reimbursed.
- How a volunteer can leave without pressure.

Do not use "passion," "exposure," or "potential future payment" to obscure an unpaid role. Do not imply that volunteering leads to a job unless a real, documented process exists.

### Equity and revenue share

Equity and revenue share are not cash compensation. If you offer them:

- Explain the split and what it is based on.
- Put the terms in writing before work begins.
- Explain decision rights and what happens if the project stops.
- Encourage the other person to seek independent legal advice.
- Do not claim a valuation, funding, or revenue you do not have.

DevSphere does not mediate ownership disputes. Keep project agreements outside the community and use qualified professionals when the stakes justify it.

## Project proposal format

Use the [project proposal template](../community/templates/PROJECT_PROPOSAL.md). A good proposal includes:

- One-sentence description.
- Problem and intended users.
- Current stage and evidence.
- Proposed stack and why.
- Work breakdown for the first milestone.
- Roles needed and skills required.
- Time commitment and collaboration model.
- Ownership, license, and credit.
- Risks and open questions.
- How to express interest.

Avoid posting only "Who wants to build my idea?" A proposal respects the reader's time and makes it possible to say yes or no.

## Finding a teammate

A useful teammate request is specific:

| Include | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Role | "React frontend developer for an accessibility-focused dashboard" |
| Skills | "React, TypeScript, basic testing; Figma is a bonus" |
| Scope | "Build three screens and connect an existing REST API" |
| Commitment | "4–6 hours per week for four weeks; schedule may slip" |
| Collaboration model | "Volunteer open-source project, MIT license, public credit" |
| Decision process | "Weekly async check-in; maintainer makes final call" |
| How to apply | "Reply with one relevant project and your availability" |

Do not ask for free work under the guise of "just a quick call." Do not request a CV containing sensitive personal data in a public thread.

## Collaboration request format

Use the [collaboration request template](../community/templates/COLLABORATION_REQUEST.md) for an existing project. Include:

- Repository and demo links.
- The problem you need help with.
- What you have already tried.
- The smallest useful contribution.
- Required access or setup.
- Review and communication process.
- Credit and licensing.

If the project is private, describe the problem without exposing confidential code or data. Move sensitive details to a private channel only after both parties agree.

## Code review requests

A code review is a request for specific feedback, not a demand for free labor.

Use the [code review request template](../community/templates/CODE_REVIEW_REQUEST.md) and include:

- Link to the pull request or diff.
- What the change is supposed to do.
- The specific areas you want reviewed: correctness, security, performance, accessibility, naming, tests, or architecture.
- How to run the tests.
- Known limitations.
- Your deadline, if any, and whether it is flexible.

Good review request:

> This PR adds cursor pagination to `/api/events`. I want review on the query plan and the boundary where the cursor is encoded. Tests pass with `npm test`. I am unsure whether the cursor should expose the internal ID; security feedback is especially welcome. No rush this week.

Weak review request:

> Please review my code.

### Giving a review

- Read the description and the linked issue first.
- Comment on the code, not the person.
- Distinguish blocking issues from preferences.
- Explain why a change matters.
- Offer a concrete alternative when possible.
- Acknowledge what is clear and well tested.
- Do not rewrite the contributor's work without permission.

## Project showcases

Use the [project showcase template](../community/templates/PROJECT_SHOWCASE.md). A showcase should explain:

- What the project does and who it is for.
- The problem and the current stage.
- Screenshots or a short demo.
- Stack and architecture.
- One difficult decision or trade-off.
- What feedback you want.
- Known limitations and the next milestone.

Do not drop a link without context. Do not claim users, revenue, downloads, or impact you cannot verify.

## Hackathon teams

Before forming a team:

- Read the event rules, eligibility, and intellectual-property terms.
- Agree on the problem, scope, and submission owner.
- Map roles and availability across time zones.
- Decide how you will communicate and what "done" means.
- Agree on credit and what happens to the project after the event.
- Leave time for the demo, README, and submission.

A team is not a hiring pipeline. Do not pressure teammates into unpaid work after the event.

## Collaboration lifecycle

```text
Idea → public proposal → questions → agreed scope
     → small first milestone → review → decide whether to continue
     → deliver / document / credit → close or archive
```

### Start small

The first milestone should be small enough to finish and review. Examples:

- A clickable prototype.
- One API endpoint with tests.
- A data model and migration.
- A documentation page.
- A reproducible bug report.
- A design token set.

### Document decisions

Record important choices in the repository or discussion:

- Scope changes.
- Ownership and licensing.
- Review expectations.
- Known risks.
- What was postponed.

### Close honestly

If the collaboration stops:

- Say so clearly.
- Leave the repository and documentation in an understandable state.
- Credit work that was completed.
- Do not publish someone else's unfinished work without permission.
- Keep private information private.

## Safety and privacy

- Meet and communicate in public community spaces until trust is established.
- Never share passwords, API keys, recovery codes, or private keys.
- Do not send identity documents, bank details, or home addresses to someone you have not verified.
- Use the platform's report tools for harassment, spam, or scams.
- Ask a maintainer for help if a collaboration becomes unsafe.
- Respect licenses and attribution.
- Do not use someone's project, code, or design without permission.

Read the [Community Guidelines](COMMUNITY_GUIDELINES.md), [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), and [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md).

## What not to post

- "Idea guy" posts with no work, scope, or commitment.
- Requests for free labor presented as a job.
- Paid roles with no employer identity or compensation information.
- Equity offers with no terms.
- Recruiter spam or duplicate posts.
- Private repositories, credentials, customer data, or unreleased confidential material.
- Harassment, pressure, or guilt-based volunteering.
- Promises of funding, users, or revenue you cannot substantiate.
