# DevSphere Community Roadmap

**Version:** 1.0  
**Planning window:** November 2026 – April 2027  
**Last updated:** 3 November 2026

This roadmap describes how DevSphere will grow over its first six months. It focuses on sustainable community foundations, free GitHub-native programs, and a consistent learning rhythm — not vanity metrics or rapid expansion.

The roadmap is a living document. Priorities may change based on community feedback, volunteer capacity, and what we learn. Changes are recorded in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

---

## North star

> A global developer community where anyone can ask a good question, find a collaborator, practice their craft, and contribute to something meaningful — regardless of where they start.

## How to read this roadmap

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Planned** | Agreed direction; work has not started |
| **In progress** | Actively being worked on |
| **Complete** | Delivered and verified |
| **Paused** | Intentionally delayed; reason documented |
| **Dropped** | No longer planned; reason documented |

Repository documentation and brand assets are complete as of the planning date. Live community configuration, programming, and engagement activities are starting now.

## Guiding principles

- **Free by default.** No paid tiers, sponsorships, or paywalled community access.
- **GitHub-native.** Discussions, issues, pull requests, and Actions are the platform.
- **Async-first.** Programs must work across time zones without requiring live attendance.
- **Sustainable.** Volunteers should not burn out to keep a program alive.
- **Beginner-inclusive.** Every program should have a clear entry point.
- **Honest.** No fake statistics, inflated claims, or purchased engagement.
- **Documented.** Decisions and processes are written down and reviewable.

## Month 1 — Foundation and launch (November 2026)

**Theme:** Make the community understandable, safe, and ready to participate.

### Goals

- Complete the public repository foundation.
- Apply the Discussions category structure.
- Publish the launch posts and welcome the first members.
- Establish moderation and contribution expectations.
- Launch the first coding challenge.

### Deliverables

- [x] Publish the README, contribution guide, Code of Conduct, security policy, and support guide.
- [x] Create the community handbook, moderation policy, member roles, and roadmap.
- [x] Design and export the DevSphere brand identity.
- [x] Prepare 12 ready-to-publish discussion posts.
- [x] Publish the first coding challenge and reusable challenge template.
- [x] Add issue templates, pull request template, and repository validation.
- [ ] Apply the 12-category Discussions configuration in GitHub.
- [ ] Move the three existing launch discussions into their target categories.
- [ ] Pin the welcome, introduction, and project showcase posts.
- [ ] Publish the official launch announcement and weekly feedback thread.
- [ ] Verify the repository's social preview and description.

### Exit criteria

- A new visitor can understand the community, find Discussions, and make a first post without asking for help.
- The category structure matches the documented architecture.
- Moderation routes are documented and reachable.
- Challenge 001 has clear submission instructions.

- Encourage members to teach what they learn.

## Ongoing commitments

These are continuous, not one-time tasks:

- Welcome new members and model respectful behavior.
- Keep Discussions organized and pinned posts current.
- Review reports and apply the moderation policy consistently.
- Verify external links and resource accuracy.
- Keep repository validation passing.
- Document significant decisions.
- Protect member privacy and never publish private reports.
- Keep every community program free to participate in.

## Success signals

Rather than optimizing for follower counts, DevSphere will look for evidence that the community is useful:

### Participation quality

- New members can choose the right category without help.
- Questions receive specific, respectful answers.
- Project showcases include context and a clear feedback request.
- Collaboration posts state scope, skills, and commitment.
- Career posts are legitimate, transparent, and free of pay-to-apply schemes.

### Learning outcomes

- Challenge submissions show reflection, not only a final link.
- Members share what they learned and cite their sources.
- Resource suggestions come from the community, not only maintainers.
- Beginners return after their first contribution.

### Community health

- Reports are acknowledged and resolved consistently.
- Moderation decisions are documented and appealable.
- Volunteers can step back without the community stalling.
- The tone remains constructive during disagreement.

### Operational health

- Repository validation passes on `main`.
- Documentation stays consistent with live GitHub settings.
- Pinned posts are current.
- The roadmap is reviewed on schedule.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low early engagement** | Quiet discussions and slow feedback | Start with a small number of high-quality posts; invite specific members to answer; measure useful replies, not volume |
| **Volunteer burnout** | Slower reviews and moderation | Share responsibilities, document processes, set response expectations, and allow roles to rotate |
| **Spam and scams** | Loss of trust and member safety | Clear posting rules, active moderation, restricted links for new accounts, and fast escalation |
| **Scope creep** | Too many programs, shallow execution | Prioritize one learning rhythm and one contribution path per quarter |
| **Maintainer concentration** | Community depends on one person | Document decisions, appoint trusted maintainers, and avoid single-person bottlenecks |
| **Category sprawl** | Confusing navigation | Keep the 12-category structure; add categories only after sustained demand |
| **AI-generated noise** | Lower-quality answers and trust erosion | Require verification and disclosure; remove repetitive low-effort content |
| **Broken or outdated resources** | Misleading learners | Quarterly link review and community reporting |
| **Platform changes** | Broken workflows or settings | Keep configuration documented; verify after GitHub interface changes |

## How the community can shape this roadmap

- Suggest a change in **General Discussion** with the problem, proposal, and expected impact.
- Comment on the current challenge or resource collection.
- Report a policy gap through the moderation or support process.
- Volunteer for a time-bound role when there is a clear need.
- Share what is working and what is confusing; both are useful.

Not every suggestion will be adopted. Every good-faith suggestion will be read, and decisions will be explained when practical.

## Review schedule

- **Monthly:** review progress against the current month's deliverables.
- **Quarterly:** review categories, policies, and resource accuracy.
- **End of Month 6:** publish a retrospective, a verified activity report, and the next roadmap.
