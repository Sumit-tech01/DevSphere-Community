# DevSphere Community Initiatives

DevSphere runs a small set of repeatable programs that help developers be discovered, improve their projects, receive meaningful recognition, and find people to build with. The programs share one rule: **participation is voluntary, evidence-based, and free.**

> **Honest status:** These initiatives are documented and ready to run, but they are not automatic. DevSphere is volunteer-run. A program happens when a host publishes it, there is a real conversation to have, and moderation is available. No member should feel guilty for missing a week, and no category is required to produce a winner.

## Program map

| Initiative | Frequency | Purpose | Primary category | Primary output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Developer Spotlight** | Weekly slot | Feature a developer and a project with consent | [Announcements](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01/DevSphere-Community/discussions/categories/announcements) for published profiles; [General Discussion](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01/DevSphere-Community/discussions/categories/general-discussion) for nominations | A consented written profile |
| **Project Review Friday** | Weekly | Give constructive feedback on community projects | [Project Showcase](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01/DevSphere-Community/discussions/categories/project-showcase) | Specific, actionable review notes |
| **Builder of the Month** | Monthly | Recognize meaningful contribution, learning progress, and project development | [General Discussion](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01/DevSphere-Community/discussions/categories/general-discussion) for nominations; [Announcements](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01/DevSphere-Community/discussions/categories/announcements) for recognition | A monthly community recognition post |
| **Community Collaboration** | Ongoing | Help members discover collaborators and build products together | [Collaboration](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01/DevSphere-Community/discussions/categories/collaboration) | Scoped project proposals and teammate matches |

The programs use the existing 12-category Discussions structure. DevSphere does not add a new category for every initiative; that keeps navigation stable and avoids empty spaces.

## How the initiatives fit together

- **Spotlight** gives a person and a project a durable, consented profile.
- **Project Review Friday** gives builders feedback before or after a spotlight.
- **Builder of the Month** recognizes progress that may not fit a single project profile.
- **Community Collaboration** turns recognition into relationships: reviewers, teammates, co-maintainers, and mentors.

A person can participate in one program without joining all four. Recognition in one program does not guarantee recognition in another.

## A. Developer Spotlight

### Goal

Make useful work and generous help easier to discover, while protecting the people behind the work. The Spotlight is a community thank-you and a written profile, not a ranking or a prize.

### Schedule

| Step | Rhythm | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nominations | Ongoing | General Discussion |
| Review | Weekly, when nominations exist | Private maintainer review |
| Invitations | After review | Private outreach |
| Interview | Asynchronous, usually 30–45 minutes | Written template |
| Publication | Weekly slot, when a consented profile is ready | Announcements |
| Index update | After publication | [Spotlight handbook](DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md) |

**Time zone:** publication slots are described in UTC. A skipped week is allowed and does not need a public explanation.

### Who it is for

Beginners, students, independent builders, open-source contributors, maintainers, designers, technical writers, testers, and community helpers. No minimum follower count, star count, seniority, degree, or paid product is required.

### How to participate

**Nominate yourself or someone else:**

1. Read the [Developer Spotlight handbook](DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md).
2. Ask the nominee for permission if you are nominating another person.
3. Copy the [Developer Spotlight nomination template](templates/DEVELOPER_NOMINATION_TEMPLATE.md) into a new General Discussion post.
4. Provide specific evidence and working links.
5. Confirm consent and disclose any conflict of interest.

**If invited:**

1. Read the invitation and the handbook.
2. Ask questions before accepting.
3. Complete the [interview template](templates/DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT_TEMPLATE.md) at your own pace.
4. Skip any optional question you do not want to answer.
5. Review the final draft for factual accuracy.
6. Approve, correct, or withdraw before publication.

### Discussion format

- **Nomination post:** one nominee or one self-nomination per thread. Use the template headings so reviewers can find the evidence.
- **Published spotlight:** a single Announcements post with a clear title, a short profile, approved links, and a note that it is published with permission.
- **Questions:** General Discussion. Do not post private information.
- **Feedback:** reply in the published thread with specific, respectful comments.

### Moderation and consent rules

- No profile without explicit permission.
- No public shortlist, score, or ranking.
- No nomination of another person without their permission.
- No private contact information, identity documents, or private messages.
- No pressure to accept, answer, or publish.
- No paid placement or affiliate links.
- A featured person can request correction or removal at any time.
- Reviewers with a conflict of interest recuse themselves.

Read the full [Developer Spotlight handbook](DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md) for the selection rubric, invitation process, and privacy rules.

## B. Project Review Friday

### Goal

Help builders improve their work through structured, kind, specific feedback. Project Review Friday is not a roast, a score, or a popularity contest. The goal is one or two changes the builder can actually make.

### Schedule

| Step | Rhythm | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Submission thread opens | Friday, 09:00 UTC | Project Showcase |
| Reviews | Friday through the following week | Project Showcase |
| Builder response | Within a reasonable period | Same thread |
| Summary | When useful | Same thread or a follow-up post |

A host may skip a Friday when there are no submissions or no reviewers available. The program does not manufacture projects to fill the schedule.

### How to submit a project

1. Choose **one** project and **one** specific question.
2. Open a Project Showcase discussion titled `Project Review Friday — <project name>`.
3. Include:
   - What the project does and who it helps.
   - A working demo or repository link.
   - The current stage and the part you want reviewed.
   - The technology stack and any constraints.
   - Known limitations.
   - The specific feedback you want: usability, accessibility, performance, code structure, documentation, naming, or positioning.
   - Whether you want collaborators or only feedback.
4. Use the [project showcase template](templates/PROJECT_SHOWCASE.md) as a base.
5. Do not post only a link. A link drop is not a review request.

### Review format

Reviewers use four short sections:

| Section | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| **What works** | Name one or two specific things that are effective. |
| **Questions** | Ask about assumptions, constraints, or decisions you do not understand. |
| **Suggestions** | Offer one or two concrete changes, with a reason and a trade-off. |
| **Suggested next step** | Name the smallest useful thing the builder could do next. |

A review can be short. "The README explains the problem clearly; I would add a screenshot of the empty state because that is where I got stuck" is more useful than a paragraph of generic praise.

### Host checklist

- [ ] The submission has context, links, and a specific question.
- [ ] The project is safe to open and contains no credentials or private data.
- [ ] The thread is in Project Showcase, not a general chat category.
- [ ] At least one reviewer is available.
- [ ] The feedback format is shared in the thread.
- [ ] The builder is invited to respond, not required to defend every choice.
- [ ] The thread is summarized when it becomes long.
- [ ] No one is ranked or compared.

### Moderation rules

- Critique the work, not the person.
- Do not rewrite someone's project without permission.
- Do not demand unpaid labor or a merge.
- Do not use a review thread to recruit, sell, or promote an unrelated product.
- Do not share private code, credentials, or customer data.
- Do not dogpile. If several reviewers agree, consolidate instead of repeating.
- Respect the builder's stated scope and constraints.
- Report harassment through the [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md).

### What good feedback sounds like

- "The onboarding flow assumes I already have a database URL. A short example with a local SQLite file would let me try it in under a minute."
- "The color contrast on the secondary button is low. I would test it with a contrast checker and keep the focus ring visible."
- "The README lists features but not limitations. Adding one known limitation would help me decide whether it fits my use case."

## C. Builder of the Month

### Goal

Recognize meaningful contribution, learning progress, and project development that may not fit a single Spotlight profile. Builder of the Month is a monthly thank-you, not an employee-of-the-month competition.

### Schedule

| Step | Rhythm | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nominations open | First Monday of the month, 09:00 UTC | General Discussion |
| Review | During the month, when nominations exist | Private maintainer review |
| Recognition post | Late in the month or the first week of the next month, when ready | Announcements |
| Index update | After publication | [Spotlight handbook](DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md) |

The schedule is a target, not a promise. A month with no well-evidenced nomination may pass without a recognition post.

### What it recognizes

- A meaningful contribution to a project, resource, or community process.
- Visible learning progress, persistence, or a thoughtful pivot.
- A project that reached a real milestone with honest documentation.
- Consistent helpfulness that made someone else's work possible.
- A contribution that is easy to miss: tests, accessibility, documentation, moderation, or onboarding.

### How to nominate

1. Use the [Developer Spotlight nomination template](templates/DEVELOPER_NOMINATION_TEMPLATE.md).
2. Choose **Builder of the Month** in the title, for example `Builder of the Month nomination: <name>`.
3. Provide specific evidence and confirm consent.
4. Post in General Discussion.
5. Do not nominate someone without their permission.

### Selection

- Reviewers use the same [recognition criteria](DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md#recognition-criteria) as the Spotlight.
- There is no quota and no requirement to choose a winner every month.
- Reviewers consider learning progress and contribution, not just shipped features.
- Reviewers do not rank people by stars, followers, commit count, or posting volume.
- A person can be recognized more than once, but not automatically or repeatedly without new evidence.
- The review group records a short reason privately and shares it with the nominee if asked.

### Recognition format

A Builder of the Month post includes:

- The chosen name and optional role.
- What the person contributed and why it mattered.
- One or two specific examples, with approved links.
- A short quote only if the person approves the exact wording.
- A note that the recognition is a community thank-you, not a prize or certification.
- Links to the person's approved project or contribution.

### What it is not

- Not a prize, cash award, gift, certificate, job, internship, or funding offer.
- Not a performance review.
- Not a ranking of other builders.
- Not a paid placement.
- Not a promise of future recognition.

### Moderation rules

- Consent is required before any name or project is published.
- Do not publish private information or unverified claims.
- Do not compare the recipient to another member.
- Do not use the recognition to promote a product or recruit for a paid scheme.
- Do not pressure someone to accept.
- Follow the [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md) for reports and appeals.

## D. Community Collaboration

### Goal

Help members find the right collaborator, reviewer, co-maintainer, or teammate for a real project. Community Collaboration is ongoing: it is not limited to a launch week or a single event.

### Schedule

| Activity | Rhythm | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project proposals | Ongoing | Collaboration |
| Teammate requests | Ongoing | Collaboration |
| Review requests | Ongoing | Collaboration or Questions & Help |
| Collaboration check-in | Optional, monthly | Collaboration |
| Matchmaking summary | When useful | Collaboration |

There is no deadline and no guarantee of a match. A slow, well-scoped collaboration is better than a rushed one that wastes people's time.

### Ways to participate

- **Propose a project:** describe the problem, the smallest first milestone, the skills needed, and the collaboration model.
- **Find a teammate:** state the role, time commitment, and what you can offer in return.
- **Offer help:** share the kind of review, design, testing, or documentation help you can provide.
- **Ask for a reviewer:** use the [code review request template](templates/CODE_REVIEW_REQUEST.md).
- **Join a discussion:** ask a clarifying question before volunteering.

### Discussion format

Use one of these templates:

- [Project proposal](templates/PROJECT_PROPOSAL.md)
- [Find a teammate](templates/FIND_A_TEAMMATE.md)
- [Collaboration request](templates/COLLABORATION_REQUEST.md)
- [Code review request](templates/CODE_REVIEW_REQUEST.md)

Every collaboration post must state:

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **The problem** | Helps people decide whether they care |
| **The first milestone** | Keeps the commitment bounded |
| **Skills needed** | Avoids vague "anyone interested" posts |
| **Time commitment** | Respects people's availability |
| **Collaboration model** | Paid, unpaid, voluntary, open source, equity, or hackathon |
| **Ownership and credit** | Prevents disputes later |
| **License** | Clarifies what others can use |
| **Decision rights** | Explains who decides when there is disagreement |
| **Communication channel** | Keeps the work discoverable and safe |

### Collaboration models

| Model | What must be explicit |
| --- | --- |
| **Paid** | Scope, rate or budget, currency, payment schedule, and relationship |
| **Unpaid / voluntary** | That it is unpaid, the expected time, credit, and license |
| **Open source** | License, contribution process, review expectations, and credit |
| **Equity or revenue share** | Written terms, decision rights, and what happens if the project stops |
| **Hackathon team** | Event rules, roles, submission ownership, and deadlines |

Do not promise equity, revenue, or a job without written terms. Do not ask for unpaid work while describing it as a guaranteed opportunity.

### Moderation rules

- No fees to join a project or apply for a role.
- No requests for bank details, identity documents, passwords, or API keys.
- No mass unsolicited messages or repeated recruitment posts.
- No harassment when someone declines.
- No misrepresentation of funding, users, revenue, or affiliation.
- No "guaranteed job" or "guaranteed income" offers.
- Disclose paid, unpaid, voluntary, equity, and sponsorship relationships.
- Use the [Collaboration Guide](COLLABORATION_GUIDE.md) for the full safety process.

## Shared moderation rules

All four initiatives follow the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), [Community Guidelines](COMMUNITY_GUIDELINES.md), and [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md). The short version:

1. **Consent first.** Do not publish a person's profile, project, or story without permission.
2. **Evidence, not hype.** Do not invent users, revenue, funding, awards, or impact.
3. **No ranking.** Recognition is not a leaderboard.
4. **No spam.** One relevant post is enough; do not cross-post or mass mention.
5. **No paid placement.** Recognition cannot be bought.
6. **No harassment.** Critique work, not people.
7. **No private data.** Remove credentials, customer data, and personal contact details.
8. **Disclose affiliations.** Say if you are the author, maintainer, employee, investor, or affiliate.
9. **Respect licenses and copyright.** Do not submit work you do not have the right to share.
10. **Allow correction and removal.** People can change their minds.

Moderators may remove content, lock a thread, or pause a program to protect the community. Decisions can be appealed through the [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md).

## Accessibility and time zones

- Use descriptive link text, headings, and alt text.
- Avoid image-only instructions.
- State deadlines and schedules in UTC when a time matters.
- Allow asynchronous participation; not everyone can join a live call.
- Do not require a paid tool, camera, or microphone to participate.
- Provide written summaries when a live discussion informs a decision.
- Respect requests for a different format or additional time.

## Privacy and consent

- Publish only the details a person has approved.
- Do not publish private contact information, identity documents, or private messages.
- Do not store consent records or private replies in this public repository.
- Do not use a nomination or review thread to collect personal data.
- If a person asks for correction or removal, act promptly and without drawing attention to the removal.
- If a participant is a minor, follow the guardian-consent rule in the [Spotlight handbook](DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md#permission-and-approval-requirements).
