# DevSphere Community Guidelines

DevSphere exists to help developers learn, collaborate, and build together across borders and experience levels. These guidelines explain what good participation looks like and how we keep the community useful, safe, and welcoming.

They apply to GitHub Discussions, issues, pull requests, reviews, comments, and any community-hosted interaction. By participating, you agree to follow them and the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

---

## 1. The culture we are building

We want DevSphere to feel like a well-run engineering community: curious, generous, direct, and respectful.

**We value:**

- **Respect.** Disagree with ideas without attacking people.
- **Clarity.** Write questions and answers that others can understand and reuse.
- **Generosity.** Share knowledge, credit, and opportunities.
- **Craft.** Care about correctness, accessibility, and maintainability.
- **Inclusion.** Make room for beginners, non-native English speakers, and people with different backgrounds.
- **Accountability.** Own mistakes, apologize, and repair harm.

**We reject:**

- Harassment, discrimination, hate speech, and threats.
- Spam, scams, phishing, and credential harvesting.
- Plagiarism, license violations, and stolen work.
- Doxxing and unauthorized sharing of private information.
- Repeated unsolicited promotion or recruiting.
- Deliberate disruption, trolling, or bad-faith argument.

## 2. Participation standards

### Be respectful

- Critique the work, not the person.
- Avoid sarcasm and insults; they are easy to misread across cultures.
- Do not mock questions, typos, or someone's English proficiency.
- Ask clarifying questions before assuming bad intent.
- Respect pronouns, names, and boundaries.

### Be constructive

- Explain **why**, not only **what**.
- Offer a next step, a reference, or a small example.
- When reviewing work, name something that works as well as something to improve.
- If you disagree, explain your reasoning and remain open to being wrong.

### Be relevant

- Post in the category that best matches your topic.
- Keep replies connected to the original discussion.
- Do not hijack a thread to promote an unrelated product, service, or channel.
- Use search before creating a duplicate discussion.

### Be honest

- Do not fabricate results, benchmarks, credentials, or experience.
- Disclose when you are sharing AI-generated content and verify it before posting.
- Clearly label opinions, proposals, and unverified claims.
- If you are affiliated with a product or service you recommend, say so.

### Be privacy-conscious

- Never post passwords, API keys, tokens, private keys, or personal data.
- Redact sensitive values from logs and screenshots.
- Do not share someone else's contact information without permission.
- Do not ask members to send you private credentials or identity documents.

## 3. Posting guidelines by type

### Questions

A good question includes:

- A specific, searchable title.
- What you are trying to accomplish.
- A minimal reproduction or relevant code.
- The exact error or unexpected output, with secrets removed.
- What you have already tried.
- Your environment and versions.

Use the **Questions & Help** category (Q&A format) and mark the reply that solves your problem as the answer.

### Answers

- Answer the question that was asked.
- Explain the reasoning and link to official documentation when possible.
- Test code before sharing it when you can.
- Do not shame the asker for what they do not know.
- If you are unsure, say so rather than guessing confidently.

### Project showcases

- Explain the problem the project solves.
- Include a repository or demo link when available.
- State the technologies and your role.
- Be specific about the feedback you want.
- Do not post the same project repeatedly without meaningful updates.

### Collaboration requests

- Describe the project and its current stage.
- List the skills you need and the work involved.
- State your time commitment and collaboration model.
- Be clear about ownership, credit, and whether the project is commercial.
- Never pressure people to sign contracts or share private information immediately.

### Career and opportunity posts

- Include the role, organization, location or remote status, and application process.
- Label unpaid opportunities clearly.
- Do not post pyramid schemes, "passive income" offers, or commission-only recruiting disguised as a job.
- Do not ask for payment, training fees, or sensitive documents.
- Remove or update the post when the opportunity closes.

### Challenges and hackathons

- Use the challenge or event title format requested by the organizers.
- Do not submit another person's work as your own.
- Follow the event's rules, eligibility requirements, and deadlines.
- Share what you learned, not only the final result.

## 4. Self-promotion and commercial content

DevSphere is not an advertising platform. You may share your work when it is relevant, original, and useful to the conversation.

**Allowed, with restraint:**

- A project you built, in **Project Showcase**, with context and a request for feedback.
- An open-source tool or resource that directly answers someone's question.
- A free community event or legitimate opportunity in the appropriate category.
- A blog post or tutorial that adds substantive value.

**Not allowed:**

- Repetitive posts whose main purpose is traffic or sales.
- Affiliate links, referral codes, or hidden sponsorships.
- Paid courses, subscriptions, or services promoted as mandatory.
- Mass recruitment, crypto schemes, or "guaranteed job" offers.
- Unsolicited direct messages to members promoting a product.

When in doubt, ask a moderator before posting promotional content.

## 5. AI-generated content

AI tools can be useful for learning and drafting. They do not remove your responsibility for what you publish.

- **Verify** code, commands, citations, and claims before posting.
- **Disclose** substantial AI assistance when it is relevant to trust or authorship.
- **Do not** post generated answers that you cannot explain or defend.
- **Do not** use AI to impersonate someone, fabricate experience, or mass-produce low-quality posts.
- **Do not** paste private, proprietary, or personal data into third-party AI tools without permission.

Low-effort, repetitive AI-generated content may be removed under the spam policy.

## 6. Code, licenses, and intellectual property

- Share code that you have the right to share.
- Respect the license of any project you reference or reuse.
- Preserve copyright notices and attribution.
- Do not post cracked software, pirated content, or circumvention instructions.
- Do not post malware, hidden payloads, or destructive scripts.
- Use code blocks and remove sensitive values before sharing logs.

## 7. Accessibility and language

DevSphere is global. English is the shared language of the repository, but it is not everyone's first language.

- Write plainly and avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Use descriptive link text and alt text for images.
- Do not correct someone's English unless they ask or it changes the technical meaning.
- Use headings, lists, and code blocks to make long posts scannable.
- Be patient with differences in time zones, holidays, and working styles.

## 8. Reporting and moderation

If you see behavior that violates these guidelines:

1. Do not escalate publicly or retaliate.
2. Use GitHub's report feature for content that violates GitHub's terms.
3. Contact a moderator privately using the route in the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
4. Include links, context, and any immediate safety concerns.

Moderators may edit, hide, lock, or remove content; send a warning; restrict participation; or ban an account. The full process, including appeals, is in the [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md).

## 9. Enforcement

Violations are handled according to impact, intent, history, and risk. A single serious violation can result in immediate removal or a permanent ban. Minor issues usually begin with a private correction or warning.

Retaliation against someone who reports a concern is itself a violation.

## 10. Improving these guidelines

These guidelines will evolve with the community. To suggest a change, open a **General Discussion** post or a documentation issue with:

- The current wording.
- The problem it creates.
- A proposed revision.
- How the change affects existing members.

Substantive changes are reviewed by maintainers and recorded in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

---

**Short version:** be kind, be specific, share what you know, credit what you use, protect people's privacy, and help leave the community better than you found it.

Thank you for being part of DevSphere.
