# Why recognition should be specific — and why consent comes first

> **Draft founder introduction.** Suitable for **General Discussion** or LinkedIn. Publish manually only. Keep the factual details current before posting.

Hi, I'm Sumit Suresh Jagtap Patil. I'm a developer and AI engineer from Pune, India. I study CSE (Data Science) at ADYPU × NxtWave and build Clariyo, a student ecosystem for mentorship, hackathons, careers, and founder community.

I started DevSphere because most of what I know came from other people: a patient answer to a basic question, a code review that pointed at the real problem, or someone sharing an opportunity they did not have to share.

That kind of help is often invisible. A documentation fix does not trend. A careful review does not show up in a follower count. A beginner who ships a first working version may not have a portfolio yet. I wanted DevSphere to make that work easier to notice.

So we are starting a recognition campaign. It has four parts:

- **Developer Spotlight:** a written profile of a developer and a project they choose to share.
- **Project Review Friday:** specific, constructive feedback on a community project.
- **Builder of the Month:** monthly recognition for contribution, learning progress, and project development.
- **Community Collaboration:** help finding collaborators, reviewers, and teammates.

**No one has been selected yet.** That is deliberate. A spotlight is not a competition, and I do not want to invent a winner to make a launch look busy. The first profile will be published only after a nomination, a review, a private invitation, and the person's explicit approval.

Here is what I am asking of you:

- If someone's work helped you, nominate them — after asking them first.
- If you are building something, bring one project and one question to Project Review Friday.
- If you want to be considered, nominate yourself. You do not need a large following or a famous repository.
- If you are not interested, that is a complete answer. A decline has no effect on your membership or future opportunities.

Recognition should be specific enough to be useful: not "great developer," but "they added keyboard navigation, documented it, and answered three follow-up questions." It should also be voluntary. No one should have to trade privacy for a thank-you.

I will be reading the nominations and the review threads. If something is confusing, broken, or missing, tell me.

Start here: [Developer Spotlight handbook](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01/DevSphere-Community/blob/main/community/DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md).

I'll go first: the thing I am trying to make better is DevSphere itself. What are you building?
