# DevSphere Moderation Policy

## 1. Purpose

Moderation protects the safety, usefulness, and sustainability of DevSphere. This policy explains how moderators handle reports, spam, disruptive behavior, and enforcement decisions. It applies to all community spaces listed in the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

The goal is not punishment for its own sake. The goal is a community where people can ask questions, share work, disagree productively, and trust that the space is cared for.

## 2. Principles

- **Safety first.** Immediate risks to people take priority over process.
- **Least intervention.** Use the smallest action that resolves the problem.
- **Consistency.** Similar behavior receives similar treatment, regardless of who is involved.
- **Context matters.** Intent, impact, history, and power dynamics are considered.
- **Privacy.** Reports are handled discreetly; unnecessary details are not shared.
- **Transparency.** Decisions are explained to the people involved when it is safe to do so.
- **Accountability.** Moderators are subject to the same standards as members.
- **No retaliation.** Reporting a concern or appealing a decision must never lead to retaliation.

## 3. Scope

Moderation covers:

- GitHub Discussions, issues, pull requests, reviews, and comments.
- Repository content, including documentation and assets.
- Profiles and behavior that directly affect community members.
- Community-hosted events and collaborative sessions.
- Impersonation of DevSphere, its maintainers, or its members.

GitHub's own [Community Guidelines](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-community-guidelines) and [Terms of Service](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service) also apply. Moderators can report serious violations to GitHub.

## 4. Prohibited content and behavior

The following may be removed or restricted:

### Safety and harassment

- Threats, intimidation, stalking, or encouraging violence.
- Harassment, bullying, or targeted abuse.
- Hate speech, discrimination, or dehumanizing language.
- Sexual content, sexual advances, or unwanted attention.
- Doxxing or sharing private information without consent.
- Impersonation or deceptive identity claims.

### Security and privacy

- Credentials, API keys, tokens, or private keys.
- Malware, phishing, credential harvesting, or destructive scripts.
- Instructions for unauthorized access or evasion.
- Sharing another person's private data, logs, or identity documents.

### Spam and manipulation

- Repetitive promotional posts, link drops, or referral links.
- Fake engagement, vote manipulation, or coordinated posting.
- Pyramid schemes, investment scams, or "guaranteed income" offers.
- Bulk unsolicited messages to members.
- Low-effort AI-generated content posted repeatedly.
- Fake spotlight nominations, purchased recognition, or impersonating a nominee.

### Integrity and intellectual property

- Plagiarism or presenting others' work as your own.
- License violations or removal of attribution.
- Fabricated experience, benchmarks, or credentials.
- Misrepresenting an affiliation, sponsorship, or endorsement.

### Community disruption

- Trolling, bad-faith arguments, or deliberate derailment.
- Repeatedly posting in the wrong category after being asked to stop.
- Publicly escalating a dispute instead of using the reporting process.
- Retaliation against a reporter or moderator.

## 5. Severity and response

Moderators consider impact, intent, repetition, and risk. The table is guidance, not a rigid formula.

| Level | Examples | Typical first response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Minor** | Off-topic post, formatting issue, accidental duplicate, mild tone problem | Private note, move or edit the post, or a light public reminder |
| **2 — Moderate** | Repeated off-topic promotion, disrespectful comments, low-effort spam, ignoring a previous note | Warning, content removal, temporary lock |
| **3 — Serious** | Harassment, hate speech, doxxing, credential sharing, deliberate disruption | Immediate content removal, temporary restriction, escalation to founder |
| **4 — Critical** | Threats of violence, sexual exploitation, coordinated phishing, illegal content, severe privacy breach | Immediate removal, permanent ban, report to GitHub, and if necessary contact the relevant authorities |

A single critical violation can result in an immediate permanent ban without a prior warning.

## 6. Reporting a concern

### For members

1. Use GitHub's report feature for content that violates GitHub's terms.
2. Contact a moderator or the founder privately using the route in the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
3. Do not publicize sensitive details or confront the person involved.
4. Provide links, dates, context, and any immediate safety concerns.
5. Tell us what outcome you need, if you have a preference.

### For moderators

1. Acknowledge the report privately as soon as practical.
2. Preserve relevant links and context; do not alter evidence.
3. Assess immediate safety risks.
4. Recuse yourself if you are involved or have a conflict of interest.
5. Consult another moderator for serious or ambiguous cases.
6. Take the least intervention that resolves the issue.
7. Document the decision and rationale privately.
8. Inform the reporter of the outcome at an appropriate level of detail.
9. Follow up if the behavior continues.

## 7. Spam prevention

Spam is one of the fastest ways to erode trust in a community. Moderators may act quickly and ask questions later.

**Common spam signals:**

- New account with a promotional first post.
- Link shorteners, affiliate links, or referral codes.
- Copy-pasted messages across categories.
- Requests to move to WhatsApp, Telegram, or a private "investment group" immediately.
- Promises of jobs, funding, or income in exchange for fees or personal data.
- Generic AI-generated replies with no connection to the question.
- Repetitive posting of the same project without meaningful updates.

**Responses:**

- Remove the content.
- Lock the thread if others are engaging with it.
- Warn or restrict the account.
- Report coordinated or fraudulent activity to GitHub.
- Block the account from the repository when necessary.

Do not click suspicious links or run untrusted code while investigating.

## 8. Enforcement actions

Moderators may use one or more of the following:

| Action | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Private note** | A direct, non-public explanation of the issue and the expected change |
| **Public reminder** | A brief comment restating the relevant guideline without shaming the person |
| **Edit** | Remove sensitive data, broken formatting, or prohibited links while preserving context |
| **Move** | Transfer a discussion or issue to the correct category |
| **Lock** | Prevent further replies while keeping the content visible |
| **Hide or delete** | Remove content that violates policy; preserve evidence privately when needed |
| **Warning** | A recorded notice that further violations may lead to restriction |
| **Temporary restriction** | Time-limited inability to participate; duration depends on severity |
| **Permanent ban** | Removal from community participation |
| **Platform report** | Escalation to GitHub for violations of its policies |
| **Emergency escalation** | Contacting appropriate authorities when there is an imminent risk to safety |

Moderators should explain the reason and duration of a restriction when safe and practical. They are not required to debate the decision in public.

## 9. Warnings and records

- Warnings are recorded privately with the date, behavior, action, and moderator.
- Records are kept only as long as needed to apply this policy consistently.
- Records are not shared publicly or with unrelated members.
- A member may request a summary of actions taken against their own account, subject to privacy and safety considerations.
- Moderators do not use private records to embarrass or pressure members.

## 10. Appeals

A member may appeal a moderation decision if they believe it was made in error.

**How to appeal:**

1. Wait until any immediate safety concern has been addressed.
2. Contact a maintainer who was not involved in the original decision.
3. Include the relevant links, context, and the outcome you are requesting.
4. Keep the appeal factual and respectful.

**Appeal handling:**

- The reviewer examines the original evidence and any new context.
- The reviewer may uphold, modify, or reverse the decision.
- The reviewer documents the outcome.
- Decisions involving the founder are reviewed by a maintainer not involved in the case, when possible.
- Repeated appeals without new information may be closed.

Appeals are not a way to harass moderators or reopen a harm to another member.

## 11. Moderator conduct

Moderators are expected to:

- Follow the Code of Conduct and this policy.
- Act with patience, consistency, and restraint.
- Avoid conflicts of interest and recuse themselves when needed.
- Protect reporter privacy.
- Never use moderation tools to win an argument, silence criticism, or retaliate.
- Ask for a second opinion on high-impact decisions.
- Correct their own mistakes openly.
- Step away when angry, exhausted, or personally involved.

Concerns about a moderator can be raised with the founder or another maintainer. Concerns about the founder can be raised through GitHub's report process.

## 12. Privacy and transparency

- Reports are confidential.
- Moderators share only the information needed to resolve a concern.
- Public moderation notes should describe the behavior, not expose personal details.
- The community may receive periodic, aggregated summaries of moderation themes and policy changes. Individual cases are not publicized.
- Sensitive evidence is stored in private channels and deleted when no longer needed.

## 13. Emergency situations

If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. Then notify the founder or a maintainer through the fastest private channel available. Do not wait for a community process to unfold when safety is at risk.

## 14. Policy review

This policy is reviewed at least every six months and after any serious incident. Suggested changes can be raised in **General Discussion** or through a documentation issue. Changes are recorded in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).
