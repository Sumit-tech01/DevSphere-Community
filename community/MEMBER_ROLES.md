# DevSphere Member Roles

DevSphere is built by its members. This document explains the roles in the community, what each role is responsible for, and how responsibilities can grow over time.

Roles are about stewardship, not status. No role is paid, and no role grants authority over another person's dignity.

---

## 1. Role overview

| Role | Core responsibility | Typical permissions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Founder** | Vision, final stewardship, repository administration | Full repository admin; final decision on direction and appointments |
| **Maintainer** | Repository health, documentation, releases, contribution review | Triage, review, merge, manage labels and repository content |
| **Moderator** | Community safety, discussion quality, enforcement | Edit/lock/move/remove discussions and comments; apply moderation actions |
| **Contributor** | Improving the repository and community programs | Same as member, plus a track record of accepted contributions |
| **Member** | Participating, learning, helping, and sharing | Create discussions, comment, open issues, submit pull requests |

A person may hold more than one role. For example, a maintainer may also moderate. When acting in a moderation capacity, they follow the [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md).

## 2. Founder

**Current founder:** [Sumit Suresh Jagtap Patil](https://github.com/Sumit-tech01)

### Responsibilities

- Set and communicate the community's mission, vision, and long-term direction.
- Administer the GitHub repository and organization-level settings.
- Appoint maintainers and moderators when the community needs them.
- Make final decisions when consensus cannot be reached.
- Protect the community's brand, values, and legal obligations.
- Review major policy changes and serious moderation escalations.
- Represent DevSphere accurately in public.

### Decision-making

The founder prefers open discussion and lazy consensus. When a decision is time-sensitive or consensus is not possible, the founder makes the final call and explains the reasoning. The founder is expected to follow the same Code of Conduct as everyone else.

## 3. Maintainers

Maintainers are trusted contributors who keep the repository and community programs healthy.

### Responsibilities

- Review issues and pull requests in a timely, constructive manner.
- Keep documentation, resources, templates, and workflows accurate.
- Triage reports and route security or conduct concerns to the right process.
- Manage labels, releases, and the changelog.
- Mentor contributors and explain decisions.
- Identify recurring problems and propose improvements.
- Help with community programs such as challenges and resource curation.

### How maintainers are chosen

Maintainers are appointed by the founder based on:

- A sustained record of constructive contributions.
- Good judgment and respectful communication.
- Reliability in review and follow-through.
- Understanding of the community's policies.
- A willingness to help others, not only to ship changes.

There is no fixed number of maintainers. Appointments are based on community need and demonstrated trust.

### Stepping down

A maintainer may step down at any time by telling the founder. Access is removed promptly and with gratitude. Inactive maintainers may be moved to an emeritus role after a conversation, without losing recognition for their work.

## 4. Moderators

Moderators protect the quality and safety of community conversations.

### Responsibilities

- Welcome new members and model constructive participation.
- Move misplaced discussions and keep categories organized.
- Respond to reports of spam, harassment, and policy violations.
- Apply the least intervention that resolves an issue.
- Document decisions and preserve confidentiality.
- Escalate serious or ambiguous cases to the founder or another maintainer.
- Review pinned posts and category hygiene.
- Help update moderation guidance based on recurring issues.

### How moderators are chosen

Moderators are appointed by the founder after demonstrating:

- Consistent respect and calm under pressure.
- Familiarity with the Code of Conduct and Moderation Policy.
- Fairness across people and viewpoints.
- Willingness to ask for help and recuse themselves from conflicts.
- Good judgment about privacy and safety.

Moderators are not expected to be online at all hours. Coverage is shared across time zones when possible.

### Moderator limits

Moderators may not:

- Use their position to settle personal disputes.
- Access or share private information beyond what is needed to resolve a report.
- Reverse another moderator's decision without review.
- Moderate content they are personally involved in without disclosing the conflict.

## 5. Contributors

Contributors are members who have improved DevSphere through accepted work.

### Contributions include

- Documentation, resources, templates, and translations.
- Bug fixes, link repairs, and accessibility improvements.
- Brand and design work that follows the brand guidelines.
- Challenge design and constructive review.
- Community programs, welcome support, and knowledge sharing.

### Recognition

Contributors may be acknowledged in release notes, the changelog, or a community spotlight. Recognition is based on actual contributions, never purchased or fabricated.

A contributor is not automatically a maintainer. Maintainership is a separate trust decision.

## 6. Members

Every person who participates in DevSphere is a member.

### Responsibilities

- Follow the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and [Community Guidelines](COMMUNITY_GUIDELINES.md).
- Ask questions with context and search before posting.
- Give credit and respect licenses.
- Protect privacy and never share credentials.
- Keep discussions relevant and constructive.
- Report concerns through the proper channel.

### Rights

- Participate in public discussions and programs.
- Ask for help without fear of humiliation.
- Receive fair, consistent moderation.
- Appeal a moderation decision.
- Suggest improvements to the community.
- Leave at any time without penalty.

Membership is free and requires only a GitHub account.

## 7. Additional volunteer roles

As the community grows, these optional roles may be introduced:

| Role | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| **Challenge reviewer** | Reviews challenge submissions and provides constructive feedback |
| **Resource curator** | Maintains a learning collection and verifies official links |
| **Welcome host** | Greets new members and helps them find the right category |
| **Working group lead** | Coordinates a time-bound project or program |
| **Translator** | Adapts key community documents for other languages |

These roles are time-bound and appointed when there is a clear need. They do not replace the Code of Conduct or the Moderation Policy.

## 8. Decision-making

DevSphere uses a lightweight, transparent approach:

1. **Discuss in public.** Proposals start in Discussions or an issue.
2. **Seek lazy consensus.** If no substantive objection is raised within a reasonable window, the proposal can proceed.
3. **Escalate when needed.** The founder makes the final decision if consensus fails or urgency requires it.
4. **Document the outcome.** Decisions that affect the community are recorded in the repository or changelog.
5. **Review.** Policies and structures are reviewed periodically, not treated as permanent.

## 9. Inactivity and removal

- Roles are stewardship responsibilities, not lifetime titles.
- After a long period of inactivity, a maintainer or moderator may be contacted to confirm continued interest.
- Access may be reduced if a role is no longer active, with a clear explanation.
- Serious misconduct can lead to immediate removal under the [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md).
- Removal of access does not erase past contributions or justify harassment.

## 10. How to get more involved

You do not need a title to contribute.

1. Participate consistently and kindly.
2. Help answer questions in your areas of knowledge.
3. Improve a resource or fix a documentation issue.
4. Review a pull request or give thoughtful project feedback.
5. Propose a challenge or community program.
6. When there is a clear need, express interest in a maintainer or moderator role in **General Discussion**.

Appointments are based on demonstrated trust, not self-promotion. Please do not campaign for a role or ask members to vouch for you.

## 11. Recognition and gratitude

DevSphere is voluntary. Every thoughtful answer, review, resource, and welcome message matters. The community is encouraged to thank people publicly, credit their work, and make participation feel worthwhile.
