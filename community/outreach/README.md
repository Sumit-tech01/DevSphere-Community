# DevSphere Outreach Templates

This directory contains the reusable, consent-first process for privately inviting a developer to the Developer Spotlight. It is not a public recognition page, a shortlist, or a promise of selection.

> **Status:** No invitations have been sent. No candidate is named in this public repository. No profile has been published. Candidate-specific drafts, replies, and consent records stay in a private local workspace.

## Purpose

The outreach process helps a maintainer send a thoughtful, personalized invitation to a developer whose work was shared in the DevSphere community. An invitation is the start of a voluntary conversation. It is not a notification that someone has won.

## Privacy and consent rules

1. **Do not publish personalized invitations.** They are for private sending, not GitHub Discussions.
2. **Do not commit names, email addresses, private replies, or consent forms.** Keep candidate-specific material outside this public repository.
3. **Do not mention a person publicly until they have accepted and approved a final draft.**
4. **Do not record a decline in this repository.** A decline is private.
5. **Do not imply selection.** The invitation says the person is being considered, not chosen.
6. **Do not pressure.** One follow-up is enough. No response is a decline.
7. **Use public information only.** Do not infer private details, employment, location, or identity.
8. **Respect the person's preferred name and links.** Ask before publishing anything.

## Where drafts live

- The reusable invitation is [INVITATION_TEMPLATE.md](INVITATION_TEMPLATE.md).
- Candidate-specific drafts belong in `community/outreach/invitations/`, which is gitignored. Do not force-add that directory.
- Keep replies, consent records, and contact details outside the repository.

The public repository intentionally does not include a candidate list. A local working list can be useful, but it must not be committed or posted.

## How to use the template

1. Read the [Developer Spotlight handbook](../DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT.md) and the [invitation template](INVITATION_TEMPLATE.md).
2. Re-check the public project links and describe only what you can verify.
3. Personalize the greeting and the specific reason for the invitation.
4. Send it privately through the person's preferred public contact route or the founder's contact route.
5. If they accept, send the [interview template](../templates/DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT_TEMPLATE.md) and confirm the exact fields they want published.
6. If they decline or do not respond, close the invitation privately and do not mention them publicly.
7. Keep consent records outside this repository.

## Follow-up

One gentle follow-up is acceptable after a reasonable period:

> "Hello [name], I wanted to make sure my earlier note about the DevSphere Developer Spotlight reached you. There is no pressure to participate, and a decline is completely fine. If you are interested, I can share the interview template and answer any questions. If not, I will close this invitation and won't follow up again."

Do not send repeated reminders, tag the person in public, or ask other community members to pressure them.
