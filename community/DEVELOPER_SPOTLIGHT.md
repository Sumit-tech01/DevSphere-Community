# DevSphere Developer Spotlight

The DevSphere Developer Spotlight is a consent-first recognition program for the people who make the community worth visiting: developers who build useful things, learners who keep showing up, contributors who improve open source, and members who help others succeed.

It is a community thank-you, not a certification, ranking, employer endorsement, award, or promise of opportunity.

> **Program status:** The framework is ready, but **no spotlights are published yet**. DevSphere does not invent members, projects, achievements, testimonials, statistics, or winners. Every published profile requires verifiable evidence and explicit permission.
>
> **Cadence:** Developer Spotlight has a **weekly publication slot**. A spotlight is published only when a consented nominee has completed the process. A week with no ready profile is a normal week, not a failure.

## What Developer Spotlight is

Developer Spotlight is a written, evidence-based profile of a DevSphere community member and a project or contribution they choose to share. It exists to:

- Help members discover work they would not otherwise find.
- Give specific, public credit for useful contributions.
- Show the range of ways people contribute: code, documentation, design, testing, teaching, moderation, and patient help.
- Create a constructive feedback loop around projects.
- Make recognition transparent enough that people trust it.

A spotlight usually includes the person's chosen name, an optional role or location, a featured project, a short interview, and links the person has approved. It does not include private contact information, unverified metrics, or claims the person has not approved.

## What Developer Spotlight is not

| It is not | Why this matters |
| --- | --- |
| A ranking of developers | Recognition is not a leaderboard. One profile does not imply that another person is less valuable. |
| A popularity contest | Stars, followers, commit counts, and posting volume are not selection criteria. |
| A paid placement | Nobody can buy a spotlight. There is no sponsored profile. |
| A certification | A spotlight does not certify skill, seniority, employability, or the accuracy of every claim in a project. |
| A prize or award | DevSphere does not promise money, gifts, credits, subscriptions, jobs, internships, funding, or investment. |
| An endorsement of a product | Featuring a project is not a recommendation to buy, use, invest in, or depend on it. |
| A recruitment channel | A profile is not a job offer or a promise of employment. |

## Program objectives

1. **Recognize useful work.** Highlight projects and contributions that solve a real problem or help real people.
2. **Recognize learning progress.** Make room for beginners whose growth, curiosity, and follow-through deserve attention.
3. **Encourage constructive feedback.** Give featured builders specific feedback they can act on.
4. **Make contribution visible.** Value documentation, testing, design, accessibility, moderation, and community help alongside code.
5. **Protect consent and privacy.** Publish only what a person has explicitly approved.
6. **Keep the process transparent.** Use published criteria, documented steps, and clear reasons.
7. **Build collaboration.** Connect builders with reviewers, collaborators, and people who can help them take the next step.
8. **Stay sustainable.** Run a program that a small volunteer team can maintain without manufacturing content or pressure.

## Who can participate

Developer Spotlight is open to DevSphere participants at every stage, including:

- Beginners writing their first project.
- Students and self-taught developers.
- Independent builders and solo founders.
- Open-source contributors and maintainers.
- Designers, technical writers, testers, and accessibility advocates.
- Data, AI, and machine-learning practitioners.
- Community helpers who answer questions, review work, or welcome new members.

You do **not** need a large following, a senior title, a degree, a paid product, or a famous repository. You do need a contribution that can be described honestly and, where possible, verified.

### Eligibility

A nominee should:

- Be a DevSphere participant or have contributed publicly in a way the community can review.
- Have a project, contribution, or pattern of help that can be linked or described with evidence.
- Agree to be featured before any profile is published.
- Be willing to review the draft for factual accuracy.
- Understand that nomination does not guarantee selection.

A person can be nominated more than once. A person can also decline at any time, including after accepting an invitation and before publication.

### Participation across borders and languages

DevSphere is global. Time zones, accessibility needs, and language differences are considered when scheduling interviews and publishing profiles. The published profile is written in English; if English is a barrier, tell the maintainers and we will discuss a workable approach rather than quietly dropping the nomination.

## Recognition criteria

Reviewers look for evidence, not popularity. The seven criteria below describe what a strong nomination can show.

| Criterion | What it means | Example evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technical creativity** | A thoughtful approach, an unusual constraint handled well, or a design choice that shows care | Architecture notes, a working prototype, a clear explanation of trade-offs |
| **Learning progress** | Visible growth, persistence, and reflection, especially for beginners | Before-and-after work, a learning log, a project that improved over time |
| **Problem-solving** | A real problem is identified and addressed with a coherent solution | User story, issue thread, tests, demo, measured result |
| **Community participation** | constructive presence in discussions, reviews, or activities | Helpful answers, onboarding, review comments, event support |
| **Open-source contribution** | A meaningful contribution to a project, with credit and context | Merged pull request, documentation, tests, maintainer confirmation |
| **Project usefulness** | The project helps a defined audience and is honest about its limits | Working demo, documentation, known limitations, user feedback |
| **Collaboration and helpfulness** | Generosity, patience, credit-sharing, and respectful communication | Reviews, explanations, introductions, co-maintenance |

### How evidence is weighed

The rubric is a guide for discussion, not a formula that replaces judgment. Reviewers use five dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | What reviewers consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Impact** | 30% | A specific, verifiable improvement for users, contributors, or the community |
| **Craft** | 25% | Thoughtful execution, documentation, testing, accessibility, safety, and maintainability |
| **Learning and growth** | 15% | Reflection, persistence, and progress relative to the person's starting point |
| **Generosity and collaboration** | 15% | Helpfulness, credit to others, and constructive communication |
| **Verifiability** | 15% | Working links, clear evidence, permission, and no inflated claims |

The seven criteria map to these dimensions: technical creativity and problem-solving primarily inform **Craft** and **Impact**; learning progress informs **Learning and growth**; community participation, open-source contribution, and collaboration inform **Generosity and collaboration** and **Impact**; project usefulness informs **Impact**; and every claim is checked for **Verifiability**.

Tie-breakers, in order: consent and safety, evidence quality, then breadth of usefulness. Reviewers may select no one if the evidence is insufficient.

### What is not considered

- GitHub stars, followers, or forks.
- Commit count, lines changed, or number of pull requests.
- Seniority, job title, employer, or salary.
- Number of posts, likes, or replies.
- Whether someone paid for a tool, course, or service.
- Whether the person is likely to attract attention to DevSphere.
- Personal relationships with maintainers.

A small documentation fix, a careful accessibility improvement, or a patient answer to a beginner can be stronger evidence than a large, unreviewed change.

## Recognition categories

| Category | What it recognizes | Strong evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Outstanding Project** | A project with a clear purpose, thoughtful execution, and honest documentation | Public repository, demo, README, design rationale, known limitations |
| **Helpful Contributor** | Consistent, specific help to other members | Useful discussion answers, reviews, debugging help, onboarding |
| **Innovative AI Project** | A responsible, well-evaluated AI application or experiment | Public write-up, evaluation, limitations, safety notes |
| **Open-Source Contribution** | A meaningful contribution to an open-source project | Merged pull request, documentation, tests, maintainer confirmation |
| **Community Helper** | Work that improves the community itself | Moderation, onboarding, resource curation, event support |

The category names describe the kind of work being recognized. They are not claims that one contribution is better than another.

## Nomination process

Nominations happen in the existing **General Discussion** category. DevSphere does not use a separate category for nominations; keeping them in General Discussion makes the process easy to find and keeps the 12-category structure stable.

### Nominate yourself

Self-nomination is welcome. It is often the fastest way to share work that no one else knows about yet.

1. Read this handbook and the [nomination template](templates/DEVELOPER_NOMINATION_TEMPLATE.md).
2. Open a **General Discussion** post with the title `Developer Spotlight nomination: <name or project>`.
3. Complete the template, including the self-nomination checkbox.
4. Provide links that a reviewer can verify.
5. State clearly that you consent to be featured if selected.

### Nominate another developer

You may nominate another developer **with their permission**. Do not post someone's name, project, or personal story without asking first.

1. Ask the person whether they are willing to be considered.
2. Explain that nomination is not selection and that they can decline.
3. Confirm which name, links, and details they are comfortable making public.
4. Use the [nomination template](templates/DEVELOPER_NOMINATION_TEMPLATE.md) and complete the consent section.
5. Disclose your relationship to the nominee and any conflict of interest.

If you want to suggest someone without their knowledge, do **not** post a public nomination. Contact a maintainer privately through the route in the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and ask whether a private invitation is appropriate. Do not include private contact details in a public thread.

### What happens after you submit

1. **Triage.** A maintainer checks that the nomination is complete, the category is appropriate, and consent is clear.
2. **Eligibility check.** The nominee's participation and links are reviewed.
3. **Evidence review.** Reviewers apply the criteria and rubric above.
4. **Conflict check.** Anyone with a personal, financial, or employment relationship to the nominee does not decide that nomination.
5. **Decision.** The nominee is shortlisted, asked for more information, declined, or placed on a future review list.
6. **Invitation.** Shortlisted nominees receive a private invitation. No one is added to a public shortlist.
7. **Interview and approval.** If the nominee accepts, they complete the interview template and approve the final draft.
8. **Publication.** A consented profile is published in **Announcements** and indexed in this handbook.

Being nominated does not guarantee selection. A category may have no selection in a given week or month. Reviewers may decline a nomination because the evidence is incomplete, the scope is unclear, or the program does not have the capacity to do the work justice.

## Selection process

Selection is a review, not a vote. The process is designed to be slow enough to be fair and small enough to be sustainable.

1. **Two-reviewer rule.** When possible, at least two people review each nomination.
2. **Evidence over presentation.** A polished video does not outweigh a broken demo; a plain README can be excellent evidence.
3. **Context matters.** Reviewers consider the person's starting point, available time, and access to tools.
4. **No quotas.** DevSphere does not force a weekly or monthly winner. A skipped slot is better than a weak or invented profile.
5. **No public ranking.** Nominations, scores, and shortlists are not published.
6. **Recusal.** A reviewer with a conflict of interest steps away from the decision.
7. **Documented reason.** The review group records a short internal reason for the decision. The reason is shared with the nominee if they ask.
8. **Reconsideration.** A declined nominee can be nominated again later with new evidence. A decline is not a permanent judgment.

The founder or a delegated maintainer coordinates the review group. As the community grows, the group can include rotating community reviewers with a clear conflict-of-interest check.

## Invitation process

An invitation is an offer to participate, not a notification that someone has won.

1. **Private first contact.** The maintainer sends a short, personalized invitation through the person's preferred public contact route or the private route in the Code of Conduct.
2. **Explain why.** The invitation names the specific project or contribution and the category being considered.
3. **Explain the commitment.** It states the expected time, the interview format, the review step, and the fact that publication requires approval.
4. **Make declining easy.** It says clearly that declining has no effect on membership, moderation, or future opportunities.
5. **No public mention.** The person is not named publicly unless they accept and approve publication.
6. **Follow-up limit.** The maintainer sends at most one gentle follow-up after a reasonable period. No response is treated as a decline.
7. **Consent record.** If the person accepts, the maintainer records consent for the specific fields to be published. Private consent records are not committed to this public repository.

An invitation can be withdrawn if the project changes substantially, the evidence cannot be verified, or the person asks to stop.

## Interview process

The interview is asynchronous and written. It should feel like a thoughtful conversation, not an interrogation.

1. **Send the template.** The nominee receives the [Developer Spotlight template](templates/DEVELOPER_SPOTLIGHT_TEMPLATE.md).
2. **Explain the optional questions.** Location, social links, pronouns, photos, and personal backstory are optional.
3. **Allow time.** The nominee can take as long as they need within a reasonable review window. There is no live deadline pressure.
4. **No recording required.** A live call is optional and is never recorded without explicit consent from every participant.
5. **Answer in the format that works.** Written answers are preferred. A nominee can ask a maintainer to conduct the interview in a discussion thread or a call.
6. **Edit for clarity, not meaning.** A maintainer may fix grammar, shorten repetition, or improve structure. They do not invent quotes or change technical claims.
7. **Review the draft.** The nominee reviews the final profile for factual accuracy before publication.
8. **One factual revision round.** The nominee can correct names, links, project details, and quotes. Substantive new claims may need verification.
9. **Stop if needed.** The nominee can pause or withdraw at any point before publication.

The interview template has 17 sections. Only the permission section is mandatory for publication; the rest can be marked "prefer not to answer" or omitted with the nominee's agreement. A profile with less personal detail is still a complete profile.

## Permission and approval requirements

Consent must be **explicit, informed, specific, and recorded** before publication.

### What the nominee approves

- The display name to use.
- The GitHub profile or other public link.
- Whether to include a country, region, or city (optional).
- The role or self-description.
- The featured project and its links.
- The technologies listed.
- The interview answers and any quoted text.
- Social links (optional).
- Any image, avatar, or screenshot (optional).
- The final published draft.

### Rules

1. **No consent, no profile.** Silence is not consent.
2. **Specific consent.** Agreeing to be nominated is not the same as agreeing to publish a profile.
3. **Exact quote approval.** A quote is published only after the nominee approves the exact wording.
4. **Private information stays private.** Do not publish a home address, phone number, private email, workplace location, identity document, financial information, health information, or private messages.
5. **Minors need guardian consent.** If the nominee is a minor under the law where they live, do not publish without a parent or guardian's informed consent. Keep the profile minimal.
6. **Consent can be withdrawn.** A person can withdraw before publication or ask for removal afterward.
7. **Consent records are private.** Do not commit signed consent forms, private emails, or contact details to this repository.
8. **No blanket consent.** Consent for one spotlight does not cover future features, other projects, or promotional use.

## Publishing process

A published spotlight is a single, well-structured post in the **Announcements** category. It is not cross-posted repeatedly or turned into an engagement campaign.

### Before publishing

- [ ] The nominee has approved the exact draft.
- [ ] Every link works and points to the intended page.
- [ ] The project description matches the public evidence.
- [ ] No private data, unverified metric, or invented claim remains.
- [ ] The category, title, and labels are correct.
- [ ] The post is accessible: descriptive links, alt text for images, readable formatting.
- [ ] The nominee knows they can request a correction or removal.
- [ ] There is capacity to respond to comments.

### Publication format

Use a title such as `🌟 Developer Spotlight — <chosen name>`.

The post should include:

- A short introduction explaining why this person was selected.
- The chosen name, optional role, and optional location.
- The featured project and a short, accurate description.
- What the person wants feedback on or what they plan next.
- A few interview highlights, with exact quotes approved by the person.
- Approved links only.
- A note that the profile is published with permission.
- A link to this handbook and the nomination template.

### After publishing

1. Add the profile to the **Current spotlights** table below.
2. Link to the published discussion from related indexes when useful.
3. Monitor the thread for questions and feedback.
4. Correct factual errors promptly.
5. Do not add the person to a mailing list, tag them repeatedly, or use their story in unrelated promotion without new permission.

DevSphere does not publish a spotlight automatically. Every publication is a manual decision made after the checks above.

## Recognition guidelines

### For the published profile

- **Be specific.** Name the behavior, project, or outcome that earned recognition.
- **Be warm, not inflated.** Avoid "genius," "best in the world," or claims that cannot be supported.
- **Credit collaborators.** A project is rarely one person's work. Name contributors and third-party assets where appropriate.
- **Respect names and pronouns.** Use the exact name and pronouns the person approved.
- **Describe limitations.** Honest limitations make a project more credible, not less impressive.
- **Avoid comparison.** Do not imply that another member is less deserving.
- **Do not publish metrics without evidence.** If a number cannot be verified, omit it or label it as the person's own estimate.
- **No paid placement.** Do not include affiliate links, referral codes, or sponsored calls to action.

### For reviewers

- **Review the work, not the person's worth.**
- **Ask for evidence, not polish.**
- **Do not penalize a beginner for a small project.**
- **Do not reward volume or self-promotion.**
- **Do not pressure anyone to accept.**
- **Do not promise an outcome you cannot control.**
- **Recuse yourself when there is a conflict.**
- **Keep nomination discussions private** unless the nominee has agreed to a public thread.

### For the community

- Ask specific, respectful questions.
- Offer feedback about the work, not the person.
- Do not use a spotlight thread to recruit, sell, or promote an unrelated project.
- Do not ask the featured person for private contact details.
- Report harassment or privacy concerns through the [Moderation Policy](MODERATION_POLICY.md).

## Privacy, corrections, and removal

### Privacy

- Publish only the fields the nominee approved.
- Do not publish private contact information, identity documents, or private messages.
- Do not publish another person's information without their consent.
- Do not use a spotlight to reveal someone's employer, location, or legal name if they have not approved it.
- Store consent records privately and keep only what is needed.

### Corrections

If a published profile contains an error:

1. Open a **General Discussion** post or contact a maintainer privately if the correction involves private information.
2. State the correction and the evidence.
3. A maintainer will update the profile or remove the affected content.
4. The correction is recorded in the thread or changelog when appropriate.

### Removal

A featured person can ask for their profile to be removed at any time. Removal does not imply misconduct; it is a privacy choice. DevSphere will remove the profile and its index entry as soon as practical without drawing attention to the removal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a big project to be nominated?

No. A small, well-documented project, a useful documentation fix, or a pattern of patient help can be strong evidence. Reviewers look for usefulness and care, not scale.

### Can I nominate myself?

Yes. Use the self-nomination checkbox and be specific about what you built or contributed.

### Can I nominate someone without telling them?

No. Ask first and confirm what they are comfortable sharing. If you want to suggest someone privately, contact a maintainer without posting their details.

### Does nomination guarantee selection?

No. Nomination starts a review. A category may have no selection, and a nomination can be declined or deferred.

### Is there a prize, certificate, or payment?

No. DevSphere does not promise awards, prizes, certificates, money, jobs, internships, funding, or investment. A spotlight is a community profile and a thank-you.

### Will you feature beginners?

Yes. Learning progress and thoughtful small projects are valid evidence. Beginners do not need to compete with experienced developers on scale.

### Do I have to answer every interview question?

No. Personal questions are optional. You can skip location, social links, photos, or any story you do not want to publish. The profile can still be complete.

### Can I use a pseudonym?

Yes. Use the name you want published. DevSphere does not need your legal name.

### Can I decline after accepting?

Yes. You can pause or withdraw at any point before publication. After publication, you can ask for correction or removal.

### Do you accept AI-assisted projects?

Yes, with honest disclosure. Describe how AI was used, respect licenses and data rights, and explain how you evaluated the result. A demo alone is not evidence of general capability.

### Can I nominate a private or commercial project?

Yes, if you can describe it honestly and provide enough verifiable evidence. You do not have to open-source it. Disclose commercial interests, sponsorships, and paid features.

### How long does the process take?

There is no guaranteed timeline. DevSphere is volunteer-run. A nomination may take several weeks depending on evidence, reviewer capacity, and the nominee's availability.

### Where do nominations happen?

In **General Discussion**, using the [Developer Spotlight nomination template](templates/DEVELOPER_NOMINATION_TEMPLATE.md). Published spotlights appear in **Announcements**.

### What if I see a profile that should not be public?

Use the private reporting route in the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). Do not repost the content or confront the person publicly.
