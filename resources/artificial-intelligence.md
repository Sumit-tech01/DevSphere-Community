| [OECD — AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles) | International policy principles |

**Questions to ask before shipping an AI feature:**

- What data was used, and do we have the right to use it?
- Who is affected, and who might be excluded?
- How will we measure harmful errors, not only average accuracy?
- Can a person understand, challenge, or override the output?
- What happens when the model is wrong, unavailable, or slow?
- How will we monitor drift and misuse after deployment?

## Suggested learning sequence

1. **Python, NumPy, pandas, and visualization.** Learn to inspect data before modeling.
2. **Classical ML.** Train baselines, validate correctly, and analyze errors.
3. **Deep learning fundamentals.** Understand tensors, gradients, and training loops.
4. **One domain application.** Vision, NLP, tabular data, or reinforcement learning.
5. **Evaluation and responsible AI.** Define what success and harm mean for your use case.
6. **Generative AI.** Learn prompting, retrieval, fine-tuning, and evaluation.
7. **Production concerns.** Monitoring, cost, latency, privacy, and rollback.
8. **Read papers and reproduce one result.** This is where depth develops.

## Avoid

- Reporting a single accuracy number without a validation strategy.
- Training on data you do not have permission to use.
- Treating benchmark scores as proof of real-world quality.
- Shipping generative AI without evaluation, guardrails, or human review.
- Confusing correlation with causation.
- Ignoring energy, cost, latency, and privacy impacts.
- Believing "the model is smart" is an engineering argument.
