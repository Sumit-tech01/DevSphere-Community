# Career Development Resources

A free, practical collection for presenting your work, preparing for interviews, building evidence of your skills, and evaluating opportunities without paying for guaranteed outcomes.

> **No guarantees:** DevSphere does not promise jobs, internships, salary outcomes, referrals, or interviews. Career progress depends on your experience, preparation, location, eligibility, timing, and hiring decisions you do not control. Verify every opportunity on the organization's official page.

## Start with evidence, not slogans

A strong early-career profile usually shows:

1. **One or two projects you can explain deeply.**
2. **A readable README** with setup steps, screenshots, architecture, and trade-offs.
3. **Tests or verification** that show you care about correctness.
4. **A short written reflection** on what you learned and what you would change.
5. **A clear way to contact you** that does not expose private information.

Use the [learning roadmaps](learning-roadmaps.md) to choose projects and the [project showcase template](../community/templates/PROJECT_SHOWCASE.md) to present them clearly.

### Portfolio checklist

- [ ] The first screen says who you are and what you build.
- [ ] Every project has a problem statement, screenshot, stack, and run instructions.
- [ ] At least one project has tests or a documented verification method.
- [ ] Links work without a login.
- [ ] Mobile layout is readable.
- [ ] No private phone number, home address, or identity document is published.
- [ ] The site uses semantic HTML, alt text, and keyboard-accessible navigation.
- [ ] You can explain every listed technology and claim.

### Resume checklist

- [ ] Contact information is current and professional.
- [ ] Every bullet describes an action, the technology, and a result.
- [ ] Claims are specific and truthful.
- [ ] Project links work and match the resume.
- [ ] The document is readable by a human and an applicant-tracking system.
- [ ] There are no unexplained gaps or unverifiable titles.
- [ ] A trusted person has reviewed it for clarity and typos.

Avoid paying for "guaranteed" interviews, resume rewrites that fabricate experience, or services that ask for identity documents before you have verified the employer.

### Prepare stories, not scripts

For each project, write down:

- The problem and who it affected.
- Your specific responsibility.
- The constraints and trade-offs.
- What you tried, including what failed.
- The result and how you measured it.
- What you would do differently.

Practice explaining the project in 60 seconds, then in five minutes. Be ready to go deeper on any decision you claim.

**Opportunity checklist before applying:**

- [ ] The role is posted on the organization's official careers page or verified program site.
- [ ] There is no application fee.
- [ ] The organization does not ask for bank details, passwords, or identity documents before a verified offer.
- [ ] The location, remote policy, eligibility, and duration are clear.
- [ ] Compensation is stated or you know how to ask.
- [ ] The application deadline and process are explicit.
- [ ] You have not been asked to pay for training, equipment, or "priority review."

## Open-source as career evidence

Open-source contribution can demonstrate collaboration, code review, documentation, and persistence. It is not a shortcut to employment, and maintainers are not obligated to mentor or hire you.

1. Choose a project you actually use.
2. Read its contribution guide and code of conduct.
3. Start with documentation, tests, or a small reproducible bug.
4. Keep one pull request focused on one problem.
5. Ask for feedback and respond professionally.
6. Record the outcome in your portfolio, including what you learned.

Use the [open-source guide](../community/OPEN_SOURCE_GUIDE.md) for the full workflow and the [open-source resources](open-source.md) for verified platforms.

## Job-search safety

- Use official organization domains, not lookalike links.
- Be cautious when a recruiter asks for payment, gift cards, crypto, or sensitive documents.
- Do not share your GitHub password, recovery codes, or API tokens.
- Verify the interviewer and role through the organization's public channels.
- Read the contract before agreeing to exclusivity, ownership, or repayment terms.
- Ask about compensation in writing; "unlimited potential" is not a salary.
- Trust your judgment. A legitimate opportunity can survive reasonable questions.
